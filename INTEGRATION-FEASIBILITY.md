# INTEGRATION-FEASIBILITY.md — Task 7: Integration Feasibility and Design Note

Assumptions stated up front (I don't know the partner landscape, and the brief says that's expected): partner systems vary widely in maturity — some expose modern REST/JSON APIs, others are batch-file-only legacy systems; a national geospatial hub, if one exists, is operated by a separate agency with its own release cadence we don't control; "utilities/infrastructure operators" means entities like the water/power/telecom regulators and road/planning authorities, who are more likely to have GIS data than transactional APIs.

## 7.1 Pattern selection — decision rule

| Pattern | Use when | Coupling | Latency | Operational burden |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Synchronous REST | A citizen-facing or officer-facing transaction needs a partner's answer *before* proceeding (e.g., identity verification during registration) | High (partner outage blocks our transaction) | Must be low (seconds) | Needs retry/circuit-breaker discipline on our side |
| Async event streaming | We need to react to a partner's state change (or they to ours) without either party blocking on the other — e.g., a business registry update affecting a corporate right holder | Low (each side owns its own queue/consumer) | Minutes acceptable | Needs a broker, schema registry, and dead-letter handling |
| Scheduled batch/file exchange | The partner literally cannot do better than nightly file drops (common with legacy government systems), or the data changes slowly (e.g., administrative boundary updates) | Lowest (files, not live coupling) | Hours to a day | Simple to build, easy to reconcile, but data is always somewhat stale |
| Consumption of published OGC services | Partner already publishes WFS/WMS/OGC API-Features and we only need to *visualise* or spatially query their data, not transact against it | Low | Depends on partner's service, seconds-to-minutes | We depend on their uptime for our map, not our transactions |
| Federated map services | We want to display a partner's layer without ever copying their data (data sovereignty concerns, or the layer is too large/volatile to replicate) | Low, but performance-coupled | Depends on partner | No data pipeline to maintain, but no offline/cached fallback either |

Rule of thumb: **if a citizen or officer is waiting on the answer, don't call a partner synchronously unless we have contracted uptime/latency from them** — prefer async or a cached copy with a "last verified" timestamp shown to the user.

## 7.2 The hub option

If a national geospatial hub carries the multi-sector physical-activity data (utilities, infrastructure, planning): I'd want, per layer — the exact CRS and format the hub publishes in (and whether that matches our national grid or needs a documented transform), a metadata profile (ISO 19115 or the hub's own profile) so we can tell provenance and currency at a glance, a catalogue entry we can programmatically discover rather than hardcode, a named data custodian per layer (not "the hub" as an entity — someone we can escalate to), an agreed update frequency, an access-control model (does the hub authenticate us per-request, or is our own gateway the enforcement point), and an explicit data-licensing statement (can we redistribute what we display, e.g. to the public map viewer, or only show it internally).

**Direction**: for physical-activity data (utility/infrastructure layers), this is a pure consume/visualise relationship into the land platform — we would not publish parcel data *into* the hub in Phase 1, since our own publish-side design (7.4) already defines a narrower, purpose-built event feed for actual subscribers, not a general-purpose hub dump.

**If the hub's update cadence is slower than our users assume**: an officer or citizen could act on a utility layer (e.g., "no underground cable here") that's actually weeks stale. The mitigation is not technical alone — every hub-sourced layer must carry a visible "as of [date], source: [custodian]" label in the UI, and any workflow that makes a legal/safety decision based on that layer (e.g., approving a build near an assumed-clear easement) must require a confirmation step, not silent trust in the map.

## 7.3 The direct option

For a direct integration with a specific utility/infrastructure provider: authentication via mutual TLS or OAuth2 client-credentials (whichever the partner already runs, since we're the smaller/newer party in most of these relationships and should not demand they adopt something new); schema and contract versioning via a versioned API path or media-type (`application/vnd.partner.v2+json`) with a documented deprecation window, never a silent breaking change; rate limits negotiated and enforced on *our* side too (a burst from our batch jobs shouldn't get us throttled or blacklisted); a latency budget stated per call site (e.g., "this lookup is allowed to add at most 2s to the officer's screen, else fall back to cached/last-known data"); retry with exponential backoff and a circuit breaker so a partner outage degrades us gracefully instead of cascading.

**What the officer sees when the partner is unavailable mid-transaction**: the transaction does not hang or silently fail. The officer is shown the last-known cached value (with its age) where one exists, or told explicitly "external verification unavailable, proceeding requires supervisor override" where the check is mandatory — the case record captures that the check was skipped/degraded and why, so it's auditable later, not swept under the rug.

## 7.4 The publish side

Events to emit: **geometry change** (subdivision/merge/boundary correction — exactly what Part A's `parcel_lineage`/audit trail already captures), **rights change** (new ownership, right ended), **land-use change**. Payload should contain: the UPI(s) affected, the event type, a timestamp, and the case reference — enough for a subscriber to know *that* something changed and go fetch current state through the proper read API. It should **not** contain full geometry, holder identity, or any attribute a subscriber isn't separately entitled to under their own agreement — the event is a notification, not a data dump, precisely so that access control lives in one place (the read API's per-consumer authorization) rather than being duplicated (and inevitably drifting out of sync) across every event payload.

Subscribers: institutions with a signed data-sharing agreement specifying which event types and which fields they may resolve after being notified — e.g. a bank verifying collateral would subscribe to rights-change events for UPIs it has flagged, not to the entire national event stream.

## 7.5 Privacy, governance and audit — more important than transport

**Data minimisation per consumer**: each institution's integration is scoped to the fields their agreement covers (mirrors the public/internal split already built in Part A — the public view excludes holder identity by construction, and every partner-facing view should be its own equally deliberate projection, not the internal schema with a filter bolted on). **Field-level restriction** and **export limits** are enforced the same way: a named view or query template per consumer, not ad hoc access to the base tables. **Audit record for every institutional lookup**: who (which institution, which credential/service account), for what purpose (the agreed purpose code from their data-sharing agreement, not free text), when, and which UPIs/fields were actually returned — this is the same `audit_event` discipline Part A applies to internal edits, extended to external reads, because "a bank looked up this citizen's parcel" is exactly as sensitive as "an officer edited this parcel."

## 7.6 Backward compatibility

For an existing, daily-used integration whose partner has no appetite for change: the new capability (hub/direct physical-activity data access) must be additive — a new endpoint/topic/version, never a modification of the contract that integration already relies on. Migration path: version the API (old version kept running, deprecation communicated on a long timeline, e.g. 12+ months' notice, consistent with the "reproduce all 25+ integrations without disrupting a single one" mandate), monitor old-version traffic until it's genuinely zero (not just "we think everyone migrated"), and only then retire it — with the retirement itself logged as an auditable, reversible-if-needed operational event, not a silent removal.

## 7.7 Feasibility matrix

| Option | Technical feasibility | Data governance | Security | Scalability | Sustainability | Institutional dependency | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hub-mediated (consume multi-sector layers via national hub) | High — standards-based (OGC), low integration effort per new layer | Medium — depends entirely on the hub's own governance maturity, which we don't control | Medium — one fewer direct trust relationship per partner, but we inherit the hub's security posture | High — hub absorbs the fan-out to many source institutions | Medium — sustainability tied to the hub agency's continued funding/mandate | High — we become dependent on an agency outside our control for currency and uptime | **Recommended as the default** for read-only physical-activity layers, conditional on a written data-sharing and SLA agreement with the hub operator |
| Direct integration (per-institution) | Medium — bespoke effort per partner, proportional to how many we need | High — governance is negotiated bilaterally and can be tailored precisely | High — smaller blast radius per relationship, but N relationships to secure and audit instead of one | Low — doesn't scale past a handful of partners without becoming an integration team's full-time job | Low — N bespoke contracts to maintain over time | Medium — dependency is per-partner and diversified, not concentrated in one hub | Use only where the hub doesn't (yet) carry that partner's data, or where the relationship needs tighter latency/contract than the hub offers |
| Hybrid (hub for breadth, direct for the few partners needing tighter coupling) | Medium | Medium-High, if both governance models are documented consistently | Medium-High | Medium | Medium | Medium — dependency spread across both models | **Overall recommendation**: hybrid, hub-first |

**Evidence needed to firm up each verdict, and who provides it**: confirmation from the hub operator of their actual (not aspirational) update cadence and uptime history — the hub agency; a list of which candidate partners already publish anything via the hub versus needing a direct relationship — whoever owns the hub's catalogue, or a discovery call with each partner if no catalogue exists; and a legal/policy read on what data-sharing agreements are actually required (versus assumed) for citizen-identifiable land data crossing institutional boundaries — the land authority's legal/compliance function, not an engineering judgment call.
